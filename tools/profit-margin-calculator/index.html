Margin and markup are not the same thing

This is the most common pricing mistake in small businesses. Both start from the same profit, but they divide it by different numbers.

  • Markup is profit divided by cost. Buy at 1,000, sell at 1,500: profit 500, markup 50%.
  • Margin is profit divided by selling price. Same example: 500 ÷ 1,500 = 33.3% margin.

If you want a 50% margin and add 50% to your cost, you only get 33%. To reach a 50% margin, you need a 100% markup. The "price for a target margin" mode does this maths for you.

What to include in your cost

Your real cost per item is more than the purchase price. Add packaging, delivery you pay for, payment gateway or card fees, marketplace commission, returns and a share of ad spend. If you spend 50,000 on ads to sell 100 units, add 500 to each unit's cost before you check your margin.

What is a good margin?

It depends on the industry. Groceries often run on thin single-digit margins and rely on volume; fashion, beauty and handmade products often aim for 50% or more because returns and ad costs are high; digital products and software can reach 80–90% because each extra sale costs almost nothing. Compare yourself with businesses like yours, not with averages.

Three ways to improve your margin

  1. Raise prices slightly and test. A 5% price rise often loses fewer customers than you fear.
  2. Bundle items so the average order is larger while delivery and payment costs stay the same.
  3. Cut the hidden costs: cheaper packaging, fewer returns through better product photos and descriptions, and ads aimed at buyers who already know what they want.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate profit margin?

Profit margin = (selling price − cost) ÷ selling price × 100.

How do I calculate markup?

Markup = (selling price − cost) ÷ cost × 100.

How do I price for a 40% margin?

Divide your cost by 0.6 (that is, 1 − 0.40). A cost of 1,200 needs a price of 2,000.

Should I include tax in the selling price?

No. Calculate margin on the price before VAT or sales tax, because the tax is not your money.
